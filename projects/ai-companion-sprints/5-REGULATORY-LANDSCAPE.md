## EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

AI Companion Sprints operates in a regulatory gap:

- **NOT regulated as mental health** (we're not therapy, not medical)
- **NOT regulated as medical device** (FDA doesn't regulate non-medical AI)
- **Regulated as consumer product** (FTC consumer protection, state privacy laws)
- **Privacy-regulated** (GDPR, CCPA, state privacy laws due to conversation data)

**Bottom line:** We're subject to general consumer protection + privacy laws, but NOT mental health-specific regulation. This is an advantage, but it means our disclaimers are critical.

## PART A: FDA & MEDICAL DEVICE REGULATION

### Is Our Service an FDA Medical Device?

**Answer: NO.**

**FDA Guidance:** The FDA regulates "devices" intended to diagnose, treat, prevent, or cure disease. If you're marketing something to treat mental health or medical conditions, it's potentially a device.

**Our status:**
- We explicitly disclaim any medical or mental health benefits
- We are NOT marketed as treatment
- We are NOT marketed as therapy
- We are goal-focused emotional support, not medical

**FDA will NOT require approval because:**
- We do not claim medical benefits
- We do not promise to treat or cure
- We are explicitly NOT a device under FDA definition

**Risk:** If we ever market with language like:
- "Treat your depression"
- "Cure your anxiety"
- "Clinical evidence for mental health improvement"
- "Medical-grade support"

...then FDA could take action, reclassifying us as a device and requiring approval.

**Our safeguard:** Clear disclaimers + marketing language that avoids medical claims

---

### What About AI in Medical Devices?

**Context:** The FDA is increasingly scrutinizing AI/ML in medical devices. Several recent frameworks require FDA oversight of AI updates, quality assurance, etc.

**Does this apply to us?** NO.

**Why:** We are not a medical device. These frameworks apply only to devices already regulated by FDA (pacemakers with AI, diagnostic tools with AI, etc.).

**What we should monitor:** If/when we expand into clinical settings or partner with healthcare providers, FDA oversight may apply. But not for direct-to-consumer emotional support.

## PART B: MENTAL HEALTH REGULATION (State & Federal)

### Telehealth & Therapy Regulation

**Are we providing telehealth or therapy?** NO.

**Why:** Therapy requires a licensed therapist-patient relationship, informed consent, and clinical responsibility. We are an AI tool, not a provider.

**State Boards of Psychology/Counseling:**
- These boards regulate licensed practitioners (therapists, counselors, psychologists)
- They do NOT regulate AI support tools
- Exception: If someone licensed is using our tool to provide therapy, they must comply with their state board

**Our risk:** Very LOW, because:
- We're not practicing therapy
- We're not licensed practitioners
- We have clear disclaimers
- We escalate crisis to professional resources

**Action required:** None (we don't need therapy licensure)

---

### Mental Health Parity & Addiction Equity Act (MHPAEA)

**What it is:** Federal law requiring insurance companies to cover mental health treatment equally with medical treatment.

**Does it apply to us?** NO.

**Why:** It applies to insurance coverage, not direct-to-consumer products. It's relevant to insurers and employers, not software companies.

## PART C: CONSUMER PROTECTION & FTC REGULATION

### FTC Act & Unfair/Deceptive Practices

**What the FTC regulates:**
- False or misleading advertising
- Deceptive claims
- Unfair practices (especially affecting vulnerable populations)

**How it could apply to us:**

**Risk 1: False Mental Health Claims**
- If we claim mental health benefits not supported by evidence
- Example: "Proven to reduce depression" (not proven)
- FTC action: Require substantiation, corrective advertising, fines

**Our safeguard:** We do NOT claim medical/mental health outcomes. We claim "goal support, accountability, encouragement" which are not health claims.

**Risk 2: Targeting Vulnerable Populations**
- If we target people with mental health conditions with non-medical product
- Example: Advertising heavily in depression forums
- FTC action: Review marketing practices

**Our safeguard:** We market as goal-focused support. Our personas (flirting, motivation) are not mental health populations specifically.

**Risk 3: Fail to Disclose Limitations or Conflicts**
- If we don't clearly disclose what we're NOT
- Example: Burying "not therapy" in fine print
- FTC action: Require clear disclosure, fines

**Our safeguard:** Mental Health Disclaimer is prominent. We repeat "NOT therapy" multiple times.

---

### Endorsements & Testimonials

**FTC Rule on Endorsements:**
- Testimonials must be typical
- Claims in testimonials must be substantiated
- Relationship between endorser and company must be disclosed

**How it applies:**
- If we use customer testimonials, they must be truthful
- If a customer says "This cured my depression," we cannot use that testimonial
- If we paid a customer to provide testimonial, we must disclose

**Our safeguard:** We don't plan to use testimonials. If we do:
- Use only authentic customer feedback
- Disclose any compensation
- Avoid mental health outcome claims
- Use only typical customers (not outliers)

## PART D: COPPA (Children's Online Privacy Protection Act)

### What COPPA Is

Federal law protecting children under 13 from data collection and unfair practices.

### Does COPPA Apply to Us?

**Answer: NO, because we age-gate at 18+.**

**Our compliance:**
- We require users to be 18+
- We do NOT knowingly collect data from children under 13
- If we discover a user is under 13, we delete data immediately

**Key requirement:** Age-gating must be real and enforced.

### What If We Lower Age Limit in Future?

**If we want to serve 13-17 year olds in future:**
- COPPA applies (stricter regulation for under 13)
- State laws apply (many states have additional child privacy)
- Parental consent required
- Cannot use data for targeted advertising
- Must implement child-safe practices
- Much higher regulatory burden

**For now:** Stay at 18+ and we avoid COPPA entirely.

## PART E: STATE PRIVACY LAWS (GDPR, CCPA, OTHERS)

### California Consumer Privacy Act (CCPA)

**What it is:** California state law giving consumers privacy rights and requiring companies to disclose data practices.

**Does it apply?** YES if we have any California residents.

**Our obligations:**
- Disclose what data we collect (done in Privacy Policy)
- Disclose how we use it (done in Privacy Policy)
- Let customers access their data (done — Section 7 of Privacy Policy)
- Let customers delete their data (done — Section 6 of Privacy Policy)
- Do NOT sell their data (we don't — clear in Privacy Policy)
- Do NOT discriminate against customers for exercising rights (we won't)

**Our safeguard:** Our Privacy Policy fully complies with CCPA. No additional action needed.

**CCPA "Sale" Definition:** Note that CCPA defines "sale" broadly to include sharing for marketing purposes. We don't do this, so we're compliant. Consumers can still request opt-out, and we honor it.

---

### European Union GDPR (General Data Protection Regulation)

**What it is:** EU law protecting personal data of EU residents.

**Does it apply?** YES if we have any EU residents (likely we will).

**Our obligations:**
- Lawful basis for processing (we have: contract + legitimate interest)
- Right to access (done)
- Right to erasure/"right to be forgotten" (done)
- Right to data portability (done)
- Right to object (done)
- Privacy by design (we attempt this)
- Data protection officer (not required under 250 employees, but good practice)
- Data Processing Agreement (DPA) with Anthropic and other processors

**Critical:** If EU resident is harmed and sues under GDPR, company could face:
- Fine up to €20 million or 4% of global revenue (whichever is higher)
- Civil liability to customer

**Our safeguard:**
- Privacy Policy addresses GDPR rights explicitly
- Attorney should review for GDPR compliance before launch (especially DPA with Anthropic)
- Consider appointing Data Protection Officer or at least having a GDPR contact

---

**Our position:** We have no specific regulatory requirement today, but we should:

## PART G: ACCESSIBILITY REGULATIONS

### ADA (Americans with Disabilities Act)

**What it requires:** Public accommodations (including websites/services) must be accessible to people with disabilities.

**Does it apply?** YES.

**Our obligations:**
- Website must be ADA-compliant (accessible to screen readers, keyboard navigation, etc.)
- Service must be usable by people with visual, hearing, motor, or cognitive disabilities
- Companion must respond to accessibility needs (if customer asks for plain language, larger text, etc.)

**Our safeguard:** 
- Website should be WCAG 2.1 AA compliant (standard accessibility level)
- Companion should be instructed to accommodate accessibility requests
- Consider accessibility testing before launch

**Not critical for MVP, but important for Week 3+ operations**

## PART K: REGULATORY RISK ASSESSMENT

### Low Risk (Unlikely to face regulatory action)

- **FDA action:** Very low. We're not claiming medical benefits.
- **COPPA violation:** Very low. We age-gate at 18+.
- **FTC false advertising:** Low. Our disclaimers are clear.
- **Professional practice violation:** Very low. We're not claiming to be licensed.

### Moderate Risk (Possible, worth monitoring)

- **CCPA/GDPR violation:** Moderate if Privacy Policy has gaps. But ours is comprehensive.
- **Accessibility violation:** Moderate if website is poorly designed. Easily fixed.
- **State mental health legislation:** Moderate if new law requires specific practices. We can likely comply.

### High Risk (Worth being prepared for)

- **Mental health incident leading to customer harm:** High likelihood at scale. Mitigated by disclaimers + crisis protocol + insurance.
- **Data breach:** Low probability, high impact if it occurs. Mitigated by cyber insurance.

## CONCLUSION

**We are in a regulatory gap — and that's good for us.**

We're not regulated as mental health (because we're not therapy), not regulated as medical (because we're not medical), but subject to general consumer protection + privacy laws. This means:

- **Low regulatory burden** (no pre-approval needed, no licensing required)
- **High responsibility** (clear disclaimers, strong security, honest marketing)
- **Manageable compliance** (focus on transparency + privacy + safety)

The key is staying in the gap: never claiming medical/mental health benefits, always being clear about what we are, and escalating crises appropriately.
