HELP / SUPPORT COPY (Detailed)

For Parents:

Screen time limits aren't punishments.
They're boundaries that give your child safety and predictability. When the limit hits,
the app pauses — not locks. Your child can ask to finish what they're doing. You can
grant extra time if something matters. The rules are yours to set and adjust.

Approval mode is for control, not distrust.
If you set activities to require approval, you're not spying. You're staying involved
in what your child picks. It takes 30 seconds to approve. Your child sees that you
care about their choices.

The dashboard isn't surveillance.
You'll see what activities your child chose and how long they spent. That's it.
We don't track when they watched, who was in the room, or whether they were bored.
We just give you enough information to know they're okay.

For Children:

Your parent can see what you do here.
Think of it like showing your parent what you're reading or what game you're playing.
You're not being spied on. Your parent just wants to know you're okay.

There's no judgment here.
If you don't finish an activity, if you try something and it's boring, if you do
the same thing twice — that's all fine. No grades. No points you're racing for.

You can always ask your parent.
If you want to try something new, if you want more time, if something feels wrong —
ask. Your parent decides. That's how it works.

FORM HELPER TEXT & DESCRIPTIONS

Age Tier (when setting up child account):
This changes which activities show up. Ages 6–9 activities are simpler and
shorter. Ages 9–12 activities are more complex and can be longer. You can
change this anytime as your child grows.

Language Preference:
Pick the main language for the interface. Your child will see activities in
this language by default. You can show bilingual content too — just ask in
settings.

Daily Screen Time Limit:
This is when the app pauses, not locks. Your child can ask you for more time.
You decide whether to grant it. You can override this anytime in an emergency.

Active Hours (when the app works):
Lighttroupekids only opens during these hours. Outside these times, the login
screen shows "Not available right now." This is a gentle boundary, not a jail.

Downtime (screen-free days/times):
Days or times when Lighttroupekids won't open at all. Many families do one
tech-free day per week, or keep mornings/mealtimes screen-free.

Approval Mode:
— Open: Your child picks any available activity. You watch.
— Approval Required: Your child suggests activities. You approve or suggest
  an alternative.
— Curated List: You build a list of pre-approved activities. Your child only
  sees those.

ONBOARDING COPY FLOW

Step 1 (Welcome):
Sets expectations, builds trust. Answers: "Is this safe?" and "Do I stay in control?"

Step 2 (Child Info):
Collects basics. Explains why age matters (not for judgment, for fit).

Step 3 (Screen Time):
The most important conversation. Explains that limits are boundaries, not
punishments. Gives real options (30 min to 2 hours, custom times).

Step 4 (Approval):
Explains three ways to stay involved. No judgment on which the parent picks.

Step 5 (Complete):
Hands off to the parent dashboard. Offers next steps and support.

Child Onboarding:
Warm welcome. Sets expectations about what will happen and that parent has
visibility. No shame. No tricks.

QUALITY ASSURANCE CHECKLIST

Every sentence passes this test:
"Would this feel trustworthy to a parent skeptical about screen time?"

✓ No hyperbole ("revolutionary," "life-changing," etc.)
✓ No vague promises ("we'll make it better")
✓ No corporate distance ("leveraging," "synergies," etc.)
✓ No emotional manipulation ("Your child needs this")
✓ No fake urgency ("Limited time!" "Act now!")
✓ No dark patterns (hidden settings, auto-renewal, surprise costs)
✓ Clear explanation of what data is collected and what isn't
✓ Transparent about what the product does and doesn't do
✓ Respects parent intelligence — explains the reasoning, not just the rule
✓ Respects child maturity — doesn't talk down to kids, doesn't hide what's happening

NOTES FOR GROVE (HTML/CSS Integration)

Format:
Each screen should be labeled with its screen ID (e.g., PARENT-01, CHILD-05).
Copy is organized by sections: HEADER, SUBHEADER, BODY TEXT, FORM FIELDS,
BUTTONS, HELPER TEXT.

Integration approach:
— Headers: Use consistent typography for screen titles
— Body copy: Keep line-height generous (1.5–1.7 for readability)
— Buttons: Label exactly as written; use consistent styling
— Helper text: Smaller font size, muted color, positioned near relevant field
— Navigation: Keep labels exact as specified in NAVIGATION LABELS section

Accessibility considerations:
— All button labels are clear and action-oriented
— Form helper text describes what each field does and why
— Error messages are specific, not vague
— Empty states explain what to do next
— No information conveyed by color alone

Mobile-first:
— All screens sized for phone/tablet first (360–480px width)
— Tap targets minimum 44×44px
— Touch-friendly spacing between interactive elements
